How Confinity actually works.

Pick a side of the loop. Ask for something to get built, or offer your skills to build it — here's exactly what to do and what to write.

Asking for help, step by step

From idea to an inbox full of real replies.

01

Open "Post a project"

Head to the post page from the button in the nav. No account wall first — write your project, then attach your email so replies reach you.

02

Describe it like you'd explain it to a friend

Skip the jargon you think you're supposed to use. Say what's broken, what you want built, or what outcome you're after.

03

Give a real budget and timeline

A range is fine. "Open to offers" works too — but a number gets you faster, more serious replies than leaving it blank.

04

Reply to who fits, ignore who doesn't

Developers message you directly. Pick based on their portfolio and how well they understood the brief — not just the lowest price.

for the "ask for help" form

One line, specific. "Fix mobile checkout bug" beats "Need a developer."

Frontend, backend, mobile, design, data, DevOps — helps the right people find it.

Timeline
This week, this month, no rush — sets expectations before anyone replies.
Description
What exists today, what you want instead, and any links, files, or examples.

Where replies land. Nothing gets posted publicly on your behalf.

Posting your gig, step by step

From "I'm free right now" to your first message.

01

Open "Post a project" and pick "I build things"

Same page, different form. It takes your skills and availability instead of a project brief.

02

List what you're actually good at

Name the languages, frameworks, and kinds of work you take on — not a generic "full-stack developer" line.

03

Link proof, not promises

Your GitHub, a live site, a case study — one good link does more than three paragraphs of self-description.

04

Say when you're free and go

Clients browsing the open work board see your gig and message you directly. No bidding, no fees taken.

What actually gets replies

Small, unglamorous habits that make a real difference on a young board.

01

Be specific, not impressive

"Fix the Safari cart bug" gets three good replies. "Seeking rockstar ninja developer" gets none.

02

Answer before you're asked

Budget, timeline, and scope up front save both sides a round of back-and-forth messages.

03

Reply, even to say no

It's a small board right now — a short "not a fit, thanks" goes a long way toward people posting again.
